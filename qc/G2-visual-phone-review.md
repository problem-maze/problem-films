# G2 Visual Review — Real Phone Close Pass

Decision: **REWORK G2 ANATOMY**

Source reviewed: real-phone screen recording `Recording_20260910_051107.mp4`.

## Confirmed improvements over G1
- Front head volume is broader and less needle-like.
- Jaw/mouth is now volumetric geometry instead of only a surface line.
- Torso carries more mass.
- Pectoral roots and caudal region have more structure.
- The full 12-second shot remains readable on the phone.

## Blocking anatomy defects

1. **Front head silhouette**
   - Still reads too dome/balloon-like.
   - Great-white wedge from snout into cheek/shoulder is not yet convincing.
   - Nose width/height relationship needs a flatter, broader blunt snout and stronger lateral taper.

2. **Jaw integration**
   - Mouth cavity reads as a rectangular/black cut-out during frontal approach.
   - Lower jaw is visibly detached from surrounding facial planes.
   - Corner transitions need a continuous cheek → jaw hinge → lower mandible volume.

3. **Ventral shell / belly continuity**
   - White ventral region breaks into hard polygonal wedges/panels.
   - The seam between body, jaw and belly is visually fragmented at close range.
   - This is geometry/topology continuity, not only a future material issue.

4. **Eyes**
   - Both eyes still read as tiny dark dots from the front.
   - Socket placement and surrounding brow/cheek volume are not doing enough anatomical work.
   - Optical material belongs to G3, but socket scale/placement is still G2.

5. **Gills**
   - Slits are not convincingly readable as five recessed curved grooves during the close pass.
   - Need stronger depth, consistent anatomical arc and integration with shoulder volume.

6. **Pectoral fins**
   - In frontal/approach view they still read too straight and bar-like.
   - Need thicker root, swept-back leading edge and a more natural hydrofoil taper.

7. **Dorsal fin / trunk transition**
   - Dorsal silhouette becomes disproportionately tall/curved in the close pass.
   - Root transition is too abrupt relative to trunk mass.

8. **Close-pass body continuity**
   - Around the 7–9 s region the shark reads as several assembled procedural parts rather than one continuous anatomical body.
   - Large fins and belly/jaw pieces expose seams instead of blending into the trunk.

9. **Peduncle / tail**
   - Departure view still reads thin and graphic.
   - Caudal root and heterocercal lobe thickness need another geometry pass.

## Scope boundary
Do **not** solve these with G3 materials. Skin, wetness, eye optics and mouth material can improve realism later, but the blocking items above are silhouette/topology/volume problems and must be fixed first.

## Next operation
Create **G2-R1 Anatomy Continuity Pass** focused only on:
- head wedge and nose proportions;
- continuous jaw/cheek/belly topology;
- eye socket position/scale;
- five readable gill recesses;
- pectoral sweep/root thickness;
- dorsal root proportion;
- peduncle/caudal thickness;
- eliminating visible assembled-part seams.

G0B runtime and G2 performance result remain locked.

G3 stays blocked until G2-R1 close-pass anatomy is visually convincing.
